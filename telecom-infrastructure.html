Work covered

A telecom enquiry should identify the site type, access conditions, tower or network package, approved drawings and responsibility split. Tower work may involve civil preparation, foundations, erection and antenna mounting where those items are included in the agreed scope. Fibre work may include trenching, ducts, cable blowing and splicing. Network build and integration requirements must identify the equipment, interfaces, testing method and party responsible for acceptance. The website does not publish standard tower heights, fibre lengths or crew quantities, so those details must be provided for review.

Information needed for a telecom package

Telecom enquiries cover very different work depending on whether the requirement is tower civil, tower erection, an optical-fibre route or an activity on a live network site. State which of these applies before anything else, because the drawings, access arrangements and acceptance route differ for each.

  • Tower type: lattice, monopole, rooftop pole or ground-based mast
  • Approved tower drawings and foundation design, with the current revision number
  • For fibre: route length, terrain, road and rail crossings, duct type and permission status
  • Cable fibre count and expected splice count per joint, where known
  • Whether the site is live, and what restrictions apply near live equipment
  • Who supplies tower steel, fibre, ducts, closures and pigtails

Tower civil and erection responsibilities

Tower civil work and tower erection are often let as separate packages. Where that is the case, the enquiry should say who casts the foundation, who releases it for erection, what setting-out tolerance and bolt projection the erecting party inherits, and who is responsible for the lifting plan and the plant used. Antenna mounting is a separate line item and should not be assumed to sit inside an erection package.

OFC trenching, duct laying and splicing boundaries

Trenching, ducting, cable blowing and splicing are listed activities, but the boundary between them still has to be written down. Trenching and duct laying depend on wayleave and permissions, which rest with the client unless the enquiry states otherwise. Blowing depends on duct continuity being proved first, and the enquiry should say who proves it. Splicing and integration end at a defined test point, and the party accepting that test should be named.

Drawings and acceptance documentation

Inspection and handover requirements vary between civil, tower and fibre packages. The enquiry should state the required records for foundation work, erection alignment, bolted connections, cable routes, splicing and network integration. Where upgrades, swaps or capacity extensions are involved, the existing site condition and any live-network restrictions should be documented. Bharat Infra Associate can then review the requested work against the location and programme without implying an operator relationship, equipment approval or technical standard that has not been supplied.

Frequently asked questions

Does Bharat Infra Associate undertake OFC trenching, blowing and splicing?

Yes. Optical-fibre trenching, ducting, cable blowing and splicing are listed activities, alongside network build and integration. The boundary between them is set per contract. Wayleave and permissions rest with the client unless the enquiry states otherwise, and blowing depends on duct continuity being proved first. Name the test point where splicing and integration end.

What does a telecom tower enquiry need to state?

Say whether the requirement is tower civil work, erection, antenna mounting or a combination, and give the tower type — lattice, monopole, rooftop pole or ground-based mast. Attach the approved tower and foundation drawings with their revision number. State whether the site is live, who supplies the tower steel, and who holds the lifting plan.
